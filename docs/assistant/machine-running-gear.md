# Machine: Running gear and lubrication

How the moving carriages ride on their guides, as drawn on every guided axis.

| Guide | Hardware on the carriage | Lubrication |
|---|---|---|
| Ground steel guide rod (crane travel) | linear ball bearing: steel flange on each carriage face, rubber wiper seal outside it | thin grease film along the rod; the wipers keep dust out and the grease in |
| Lead screw (every spindle axis) | flanged bronze nut bolted to the carriage face, wiper on the far side | grease on the thread |
| Aluminium profile (crane lift mast, robot columns, robot arm rails) | UHMW-PE guide shoes hugging the profile at each face | none: polymer shoes run dry, which is why they are used on profiles |

Axes with this running gear: crane travel (rod and screw), crane lift (four mast tubes and screw),
fork (screw), robot plunge (four columns and screw), robot reach (two rails and screw).

The hardware is found from the geometry: wherever a rod, a screw or a profile runs through a part
that moves relative to it along that guide, that part rides on it. It is drawn at the carriage
faces and stands at most 4.5 mm proud of them.

Wear: the crane's travel spindle and nut is a monitored wear part (actuator current and move
time grow as the nut wears or the grease dries). Re-greasing is part of preventive maintenance.
