Physical AI architecture

  • One time base. The loop drive's encoder is the master axis; every station latches it (puck-edge sensors) and runs as its own agent on its own I/O node.
  • The product carries its recipe. NFC tag per puck: flavour (band row) + batch + bake log (zone temperatures, IR1) written at the transfer, QC verdict at the hood, read before the pickers.
  • Vision QC (CAM1 + ft colour sensor in a light-tight hood): flavour, burn, crack, size. Trained on images rendered from this CAD twin (sim-to-real), fine-tuned on the real line; verdict budget 0.5 s vs 5.5 s of travel to the kicker.
  • Tracking pickers (CAM2 upstream): cookie pose + puck phase; the delta IK is part of this model and every pick/place point is proven reachable.
  • Health: current + encoder per motor, zone thermocouples; anomalies feed the scheduler.
  • No self-throttling at the depositor: the band holds ~9 min of product, so stopping the dough when both pickers fail leaves a 9-min hole after they recover. Simulated (10-min double outage): throttled 0.62 vs keep-baking 0.77 - the agent keeps baking and lets the crumb pan take the overflow.
  • AMR fleet (2, N+1): every buffer raises a task with a deadline = the autonomy it has left; earliest deadline first; one visit per port serves all its pending tasks. Proven: ONE robot's worst case (all 10 port tasks queued) beats every deadline; simulated: one robot carries an 8 h shift, the fleet may vanish for 15 min without loss.

Packaging and storage - how a round cookie ends up in a rectangular pack

  1. An empty pocket tray (160 x 55 x 24, 3 round pockets D46.5 x 21) drops from a nested magazine (corner guides, escapement forks) onto the lane.
  2. A delta sets each round cookie straight down into a round pocket: 0.75 mm radial clearance against a 0.60 mm placement error budget.
  3. The top sealer seals a peelable film on the 3 mm flange during the lane index; the cookie stays 1 mm under it. Twin film reels with auto-splice last 11.8 h per lane.
  4. The stacker pushes each sealed pack up through spring pawls into the open bottom of a hollow cassette; the pawls hold the stack by its flange. The cassette's top is open: the first pack in has risen to the top, so packs leave first in, first out. The lugs sit on the end walls so the outlet stays clear (checked).
  5. A full cassette (27 packs) is carried into the out airlock; with the inner door locked the AMR swaps it for an empty one (30 s, covered by the pack waiting on the stacker).

Proven on the exact CAD (line_cad.containment): every cookie sits in its pocket or tube, every stack on its pawls, zero overlap, and nothing blocks a cassette outlet. Negative tests (pocket too small, stack off its pawls, a handle across the outlet) are all caught.

Open items (honest)

  • Every parameter tagged [assumed] (chain, delta arm lengths, pick time, bake/cool times, puck) must be validated on hardware.
  • A fleet outage longer than the shortest buffer (~17 min) runs the box magazines dry first: taller box magazines or a second box port are the next lever.
  • The band cannot stop with the oven hot: a picker outage longer than the loop's empty pucks last sends cookies into the crumb pan (counted as band loss); delta C is a single transfer (a second one does not fit the bend).
  • The food data in oven.py (dough water, conductivities, browning constants, fan heat-transfer coefficients) are [assumed] - MEASURE on the first bake; see blueprints/M3_thermal.svg.
  • The delta arms are checked for reach, not yet swept for collision with the portal over the whole workspace; lane indexing is simplified (independent box spots).
  • Isometric pictorials are schematic (painter's order); the three orthographic views are exact.
